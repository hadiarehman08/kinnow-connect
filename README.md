# Kinnow Connect

KinnowLink — Complete Responsive Agricultural Marketplace Web App



Build a complete, responsive, mobile-first agricultural marketplace web app called KinnowLink from scratch. It connects citrus growers (especially kinnow growers) with fruit buyers, wholesalers, retailers, exporters, and other produce buyers. The app must feel like a polished, trustworthy, modern marketplace product, not a generic admin dashboard or template. Build the actual interactive frontend with realistic demo data and functional navigation, forms, filters, matching, offers, and messaging. Clearly distinguish demo functionality from real backend functionality. Do not claim real transactions, verified users, or live data unless implemented.



PRODUCT PURPOSE



KinnowLink helps growers list available produce and helps buyers discover suitable lots. It also helps buyers publish requirements, growers respond with offers, and both sides communicate and track deal progress. The product has two user roles: Grower and Buyer. A user chooses a role at onboarding and can switch roles later from Profile. The app's navigation, dashboard, and primary actions must adapt to the selected role.



DESIGN DIRECTION



Create a premium, distinctive, citrus-inspired agricultural technology product using a refined combination of modern editorial design, soft glassmorphism, organic forms, natural lighting, and minimal marketplace UI.



The overall visual identity should feel like a carefully art-directed agritech startup: warm, confident, contemporary, natural, sophisticated, and approachable. The experience should feel cohesive from the welcome screen through every dashboard, form, listing, matching screen, message thread, and profile page.



The visual direction should be inspired by the approved KinnowLink concept: a warm ivory canvas, a large translucent frosted-glass hero card, oversized abstract citrus forms, soft blurred orange and yellow lighting, delicate botanical details, subtle curved outlines, and a restrained forest-green and citrus-orange identity.



This is a complete app-wide visual system, not a homepage-only treatment. Translate the same design language into every screen while adapting the amount of decoration to the task and information density of each page.

Keep the interface predominantly light, with dark green used for contrast and hierarchy rather than making the application a dark-themed product.



Maintain accessible contrast, especially on buttons, small labels, muted text, status indicators, and translucent surfaces.



2. Premium citrus-inspired hero design



Make the onboarding and welcome experience the most visually memorable part of the application.



Use a spacious warm-ivory background with a large rounded translucent hero card as the main visual focus.



The hero card should feature:



- A softly frosted, semi-transparent surface.

- A subtle light border.

- Gentle depth and diffused shadows.

- A refined rounded silhouette.

- Warm ambient lighting.

- Subtle organic curved outlines.

- Carefully controlled transparency and background blur.

- Plenty of negative space around the content.



Behind and around the card, introduce oversized abstract citrus-inspired elements:



- Large softly lit orange and yellow spheres.

- Subtle orange-to-yellow color transitions within decorative shapes.

- Blurred botanical green foliage.

- Delicate curved lines inspired by citrus slices and natural forms.

- Soft overlapping organic shapes.

- Gentle highlights that create a premium, dimensional appearance.



Use these decorative elements as a composition around the card rather than placing them randomly. They should create depth and visual interest while keeping the actual interface readable.



The hero should feel editorial and premium, with strong typographic hierarchy and an intentional relationship between the brand, headline, supporting copy, and role-selection cards.



Do not use a large generic stock photograph as the default hero background. Prefer carefully composed abstract 3D citrus forms, subtle foliage, soft lighting, and refined graphic shapes. Use real produce photography only where it adds meaningful product context.



3. Glassmorphism and surface treatment



Use glassmorphism as a distinctive material within the design system, but apply it selectively and purposefully.



Hero panels and prominent welcome cards:



- Frosted, translucent ivory surfaces.

- Soft backdrop blur.

- Thin light borders.

- Gentle shadows.

- Subtle warm reflections.

- Large, consistent corner radii.



Secondary cards:



- Mostly opaque white, pale mint, or pale warm yellow surfaces.

- Soft shadows and restrained borders.

- Small translucent decorative details where appropriate.

- Clean and readable content areas.



Functional dashboard panels:



- Prioritize legibility and usability.

- Use crisp, mostly opaque surfaces.

- Reserve glass effects for selected featured panels, overlays, or visual accents.

- Avoid transparency behind dense text, tables, charts, forms, or important controls.



Never allow background shapes, blur, shadows, or translucent layers to reduce the contrast or usability of the interface.



4. Typography and editorial hierarchy



Use a contemporary, high-quality sans-serif typeface such as Manrope, Inter, or DM Sans.



Use an editorial approach to hierarchy:



- Large, bold, confident headlines on welcome and authentication screens.

- Strong but restrained section headings throughout the app.

- Clear, medium-weight card titles.

- Readable body copy with comfortable line height.

- Small uppercase labels with generous letter spacing for contextual information.

- Consistent typographic scale and spacing.



On the welcome screen, use large dark-green headline typography and selectively highlight key words in citrus orange. Use short, carefully composed lines rather than dense paragraphs.



On dashboards and functional pages, reduce headline scale as necessary to keep the experience practical and information-dense without becoming cramped.



Use typography, spacing, and alignment to establish hierarchy rather than relying on excessive borders, gradients, or decorative elements.



5. Organic forms and citrus visual language



Introduce a consistent graphic language inspired by kinnow fruit, citrus leaves, orchards, and natural movement.



Use:

Use the shapes to guide attention, frame important content, and create a recognizable KinnowLink identity.



Avoid cartoonish fruit illustrations, excessive leaf decorations, busy patterns, and literal citrus imagery on every card.



The visual language should be sophisticated and restrained, closer to premium product branding than a playful grocery application.

Grower:

Buyer:

Both experiences must clearly belong to the same product. Do not create two unrelated themes or different interface systems.

8. Dashboard visual language

RESPONSIVE LAYOUT



Design mobile-first. On mobile, use a fixed bottom navigation bar with icons and short labels, safe-area spacing, and enough content padding so it never covers buttons or lists. On desktop, use a clean top header or sidebar/navigation layout appropriate to the screen width, without duplicating navigation awkwardly. Forms must be easy to complete on a phone, with clear labels, sensible input types, validation, and readable spacing. Include loading, empty, success, and error states where relevant.



BRAND AND APP SHELL



Use the KinnowLink name and a simple citrus-link brand mark in the header. Keep the logo consistent across onboarding, app header, and auth screens. Provide a compact header with page title/context, notifications affordance if implemented, and profile access. Use a consistent app shell across all pages.



ONBOARDING AND ROLE SELECTION



Create a welcome screen explaining the product in one concise sentence. Present two large role cards: “I’m a Grower” and “I’m a Buyer”. Grower explanation: list kinnow lots and connect with buyers. Buyer explanation: discover produce and post sourcing requirements. Selecting a card sets the active role and opens the corresponding home dashboard. Persist the selected role in app state/local persistence where appropriate. Provide a way to switch role from Profile, with a clear confirmation or explanation if switching changes navigation context. Do not force the user through onboarding on every visit once a role is selected.



ROLE-BASED NAVIGATION



Grower bottom navigation, in this order: Home, My Lots, Buyer Needs, Matches, Messages, Profile.



Buyer bottom navigation, in this order: Home, Find Lots, Requirements, Matches, Messages, Profile.



Use a clear active state, intuitive Lucide icons, accessible labels, and consistent spacing. Every tab must open a real page. “Matches” is a distinct destination for personalized opportunities, not just a duplicate marketplace list. “Messages” must be a first-class destination for both roles.



GROWER EXPERIENCE



Grower Home dashboard should show a welcoming greeting, concise overview, and primary CTA “Post a Lot”. Include useful summary cards such as Active Lots, New Matches, Incoming Offers, and Unread Messages, using demo counts derived from demo data. Add a compact “Your next steps” or activity area and a preview of relevant buyer needs/matches. Avoid decorative metrics that don't lead to action.



My Lots page: show the grower's own produce listings with search/filter controls and clear status chips such as Draft, Active, Paused, Sold/Closed. Each lot card should show variety, quantity, grade/quality, location, expected readiness/harvest date, asking price if supplied, and last updated. Include actions to view details, edit, pause/reactivate, and close a listing. Make status changes update the UI. Include a meaningful empty state with a “Post a Lot” CTA.



Post a Lot form: fields for produce/variety (default to Kinnow but allow other citrus if suitable), quantity and unit, grade/quality, location, availability/readiness date, price or “Open to offers”, description, and optional photos. Use appropriate validation and clear required/optional labels. On submit, create a listing in app state and show a success confirmation; the new lot should appear in My Lots and be eligible for matching. If there is no backend, label the data as demo/local and don't imply it is published to real buyers.



Buyer Needs page: growers can browse active buyer requirements. Include filters for variety, quantity, grade, location, needed-by date, and price range when present. Cards should show buyer's requested produce, amount, specifications, location, date, and a “View need” / “Make offer” action. Include a detail view with complete information and a clear offer CTA.



BUYER EXPERIENCE



Buyer Home dashboard should show a welcoming greeting, primary CTA “Find Lots” and secondary CTA “Post a Requirement”. Include useful summary cards such as Saved/Shortlisted Lots, Active Requirements, Incoming Offers, and Unread Messages, derived from demo data. Show recently added lots and a preview of relevant matches with reasons.



Find Lots page: searchable, filterable marketplace of grower listings. Filters should include variety, quantity, grade, location, availability date, and price where available. Include sort options such as newest, quantity, and price. Each lot card should clearly show variety, quantity/unit, grade, location, availability, asking price/open-to-offers, grower profile summary, and actions to view details, save, or contact/make an offer. Filters and sorting must actually change visible results. Include useful empty states and reset-filter actions.



Lot detail page: show full listing specifications, photos if available, location, readiness, pricing terms, grower profile/business details, listing status, and relevant actions: Save, Message Grower, and Make Offer. Make these actions functional in the demo experience. Clearly show whether a price is fixed, indicative, or open to offers; don't invent missing details.



Requirements page: buyers can create and manage sourcing requirements. Include status chips such as Draft, Active, Paused, Fulfilled/Closed. Each requirement should show requested variety, quantity, quality/grade, delivery/location, needed-by date, target price/budget if supplied, and description. Include edit, pause/reactivate, and close actions. Provide a clear empty state with “Post a Requirement”.



Post a Requirement form: fields for produce/variety, quantity/unit, desired grade/quality, destination/location, needed-by date, target price or “Open to offers”, delivery preferences if relevant, and notes. Validate required fields. On submit, add the requirement to the buyer's Requirements page and make it eligible for matching. Show confirmation. Without a backend, clearly treat this as a local demo action.



MATCHES — IMPORTANT CORE FEATURE



Create a dedicated Matches page for each role. It must show opportunities generated from the user's own active listings or active requirements, not a generic feed. Explain in plain language why each match is relevant. Use matching dimensions such as produce/variety compatibility, quantity compatibility (including partial matches), grade/quality, location/delivery feasibility, availability/needed-by dates, and price only when both sides provide comparable price information.



For growers: match their active lots against active buyer requirements. For buyers: match their active requirements against active grower lots. Show the paired listing/requirement summary, compatibility details, quantity fit, location, date fit, and a clear next action such as View, Message, or Make Offer. Use statuses such as New, Viewed, Contacted, Negotiating, and Closed where applicable. Never fabricate match scores, guarantees, or claims of algorithmic certainty. If a numeric compatibility indicator is used, define how it is calculated and label it as an estimate; preferably use transparent textual reasons instead of arbitrary scores. Distinguish exact fit from partial quantity fit and clearly explain mismatches or unknown fields. If there are no matches, explain how to create or improve a listing/requirement and provide the relevant CTA. Keep Matches separate from browsing: Buyer Needs/Find Lots are discovery pages; Matches are personalized suggestions based on the user's own posts; Offers are responses and negotiation activity.

OFFERS AND NEGOTIATION

Provide an Offers area accessible from relevant listing/requirement detail and dashboard previews. If Offers is not a bottom-nav destination, make it accessible from Home, Matches, listing/requirement detail, and Messages, with a clear route and page title. Show incoming and sent offers, linked to the relevant lot/requirement, quantity, offered price/terms if provided, sender, date, and status (Pending, Accepted, Declined, Countered, Withdrawn, Completed). Provide detail views and functional demo actions to accept, decline, counter, or withdraw where appropriate. Keep action states consistent across screens. Do not represent an accepted offer as a completed real-world sale.

MESSAGES

Create a functional Messages inbox for both roles, with conversation list, participant/business name, linked lot or requirement context, last message preview, timestamp, unread indicator, and empty state. Opening a conversation shows a chat-style thread and a composer with send action. Support demo messages and update the conversation preview/unread state when sending/opening. Include context links back to the associated listing, requirement, or offer. If there is no backend, clearly label messages as demo/local and don't imply delivery to a real person.

PROFILE

Create a Profile page with name, business/farm name, role, location, contact details, and relevant business information. Allow editing profile fields in the demo app. Include role switching, notification preferences if implemented, help/about, and sign out/reset demo option. Do not require sensitive personal information unnecessarily. Make clear which profile details are visible to other marketplace users in a real deployment.

DATA MODEL AND FUNCTIONALITY

Use a coherent data model for users, lots, requirements, matches, offers, conversations, and messages. Use realistic but clearly fictional demo records so the marketplace is populated on first launch and all pages can be explored. Keep demo data consistent across pages and actions. Use shared state or a simple local persistence layer so creating/editing listings, requirements, offers, messages, saved lots, and status changes update all relevant screens. Avoid hardcoded counters that contradict the displayed records. Include a reset-demo-data action. Make the app usable without login for prototype exploration, but structure it so real authentication and database integration can be added later.

FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

All navigation items work. All primary CTAs work. Forms validate and submit. Search, filters, sorting, tabs, save/shortlist, status changes, offer actions, and message sending work in the frontend. Use clear feedback for success and validation errors. Prevent dead buttons and placeholder links. Use reusable components and clean code organization. Ensure mobile layout does not overflow horizontally and bottom navigation does not obscure content. Include accessible focus states, semantic labels, and sufficient contrast.

TECHNICAL DIRECTION

Use React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and a suitable icon library such as Lucide. Use a clean component structure and reusable UI primitives. Choose routing appropriate to the project and ensure browser refresh/deep links behave sensibly. Keep data logic separate from presentation where practical. Do not add unnecessary dependencies. If a backend is not configured, implement a self-contained demo with local state/persistence and clearly identify it as a prototype. Do not claim authentication, live messaging, payments, verification, or production data storage unless actually implemented.

BUILD ORDER

Establish visual system, app shell, onboarding, role state, and responsive navigation.

Build Grower and Buyer dashboards and all core pages.

Implement data model, demo records, forms, and state synchronization.

Implement matching logic with transparent reasons and partial quantity handling.

Implement offers and messages.

Polish responsive behavior, empty/loading/error states, accessibility, and visual consistency.

FINAL QUALITY CHECK

Before finishing, test the complete user journey for both roles: choose role → dashboard → create lot/requirement → see it in own listings → see relevant matching opportunities → open detail → send an offer or message → update status → verify all related screens stay consistent. Test role switching, filters, empty states, mobile navigation, form validation, and reset demo data. Do not stop at a static mockup. Deliver a cohesive, clickable, functional prototype with realistic content and a refined KinnowLink visual identity.

Add Dark Mode to KinnowLink



Implement a complete, polished Dark Mode for the existing KinnowLink app. This is an enhancement to the current application, not a redesign.



1. Core Requirement



Add a theme system with three options:



- Light Mode — the existing default appearance.

- Dark Mode — a carefully designed dark version of the existing visual identity.

- System — automatically follows the user's device theme.



Add a theme toggle in the Profile/Settings area. If appropriate, also provide a compact theme control in the app's main interface without overcrowding the navigation.



2. Preserve the Existing App



Do not change or remove:



- Existing layouts, page structures, navigation, or role-based experiences.

- Grower and Buyer dashboards, lots, requirements, matches, offers, messages, and profiles.

- Existing interactions, demo data, filters, forms, and local persistence.

- The current citrus-inspired glassmorphism design direction.



Dark mode must work consistently across every screen and component, including modals, dropdowns, forms, cards, charts, empty states, and navigation.



3. Dark Mode Visual Identity



Create a premium, warm, citrus-inspired dark theme. Avoid pure black backgrounds and harsh white text.



Suggested dark palette:



- Main background: "#111916" — deep green-black.

- Secondary background: "#17221D" — dark forest.

- Card surface: "#1D2B24" — elevated green.

- Elevated surface: "#26372E".

- Primary text: "#F4F2E9" — warm ivory.

- Secondary text: "#B8C4B9".

- Muted text: "#87968B".

- Primary green: "#8FC6A0".

- Citrus orange: "#FFAA45".

- Soft citrus yellow: "#EACB70".

- Borders: "#34463A".



Use these as a coherent starting palette and adjust individual colors when necessary to maintain accessibility and visual hierarchy.



4. Glassmorphism in Dark Mode



Adapt the existing frosted-glass aesthetic rather than simply inverting its colors.



- Use translucent deep-green surfaces with subtle light borders.

- Apply restrained background blur to hero panels and selected floating elements.

- Use soft, low-opacity green or amber highlights.

- Preserve the organic citrus-inspired visual accents, but reduce their brightness and contrast where needed.

- Keep cards and functional content readable; avoid excessive transparency behind text.



The main hero area should retain its visual character, with a dark frosted-glass treatment and subtle citrus accents.



5. Component-Level Adaptation



Update all components for dark mode:



Navigation



- Dark forest navigation surface.

- Warm ivory labels and icons.

- A clear, accessible active state using the primary green or citrus accent.

- Preserve existing navigation behavior and layout.



Cards and dashboards



- Distinguish the page background, card surface, and elevated cards.

- Use subtle borders and restrained shadows.

- Maintain clear separation between data groups and dashboard sections.



Lots, buyer requirements, and matches



- Keep variety, quantity, grade, location, dates, and status information easy to scan.

- Ensure badges remain legible and visually distinct.

- Preserve matching explanations and compatibility indicators.

- Use colors consistently for statuses, without relying on color alone.



Buttons and actions



- Maintain clear primary, secondary, outline, and destructive button styles.

- Provide visible hover, pressed, focused, and disabled states.

- Preserve the existing action hierarchy.



Forms and filters



- Dark input surfaces with clear borders and readable placeholder text.

- Ensure dropdowns, date pickers, checkboxes, radio buttons, and validation messages work in both themes.

- Make selected and focused states easy to identify.



Messages and offers



- Clearly distinguish sent and received messages.

- Preserve readable timestamps, conversation previews, and offer details.

- Ensure accept, decline, counter, and withdraw actions remain visually understandable.



Charts and data visualization



- Adapt chart backgrounds, grid lines, labels, tooltips, and legends.

- Maintain sufficient contrast without introducing unnecessary colors.



Modals and overlays



- Use dark elevated surfaces, subtle borders, and appropriate backdrops.

- Ensure menus and dialogs are not hidden behind other elements.



6. Theme Switching and Persistence



Implement a reliable theme system:



1. Add Light, Dark, and System options in Profile/Settings.

2. Apply the selected theme immediately when the user changes it.

3. Persist the user's selection using the app's existing local persistence approach.

4. On first visit, use the device's system preference unless the existing app explicitly sets a different default.

5. When System is selected, respond to changes in the device's theme.

6. Prevent a noticeable flash of the wrong theme during app startup, where technically feasible.

7. Keep the chosen theme when navigating between pages and refreshing the app.



Use a centralized theme provider or equivalent shared state. Avoid implementing separate, inconsistent theme logic inside individual pages.



7. Technical Implementation



- Use the app's existing React, TypeScript, and Tailwind setup.

- Prefer CSS variables/design tokens for theme-dependent colors.

- Use a consistent theme class or data attribute on the root element.

- Replace hardcoded theme-dependent colors with semantic tokens where necessary.

- Preserve existing components and reuse them wherever possible.

- Do not duplicate entire pages just to support dark mode.

- Do not introduce unnecessary dependencies.



8. Accessibility and Visual Quality



- Maintain WCAG AA contrast for normal text wherever applicable.

- Keep focus indicators clearly visible.

- Do not use color as the only way to communicate status or errors.

- Ensure icons remain distinguishable and controls remain easy to use.

- Check dark mode on mobile and desktop layouts.

- Avoid overly bright surfaces, glowing borders, excessive gradients, and pure-black backgrounds.



9. Final Quality Check



After implementation, verify:



- Every screen works in Light, Dark, and System modes.

- The theme toggle works and the selection persists after refresh.

- System mode responds to device theme changes.

- No white flashes, unreadable text, invisible borders, or light-only components remain.

- All existing navigation, matching, forms, filters, offers, and messaging interactions still work.

- The app retains its existing visual identity and functionality.



Important: Keep Light Mode visually unchanged. Dark Mode should feel like the same KinnowLink product at night—not a different app. Do not modify business logic, matching logic, demo content, or page structure as part of this task.
